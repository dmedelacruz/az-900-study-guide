## Imagine Renting Space in a World-Class Managed Office Tower

Picture the difference between owning an old office building and renting a floor in a modern, professionally managed skyscraper downtown.

When you own the building, everything is your problem. If a pipe bursts and floods the server room, you call the plumber. If the backup generator fails during a blackout, your business goes dark. If you suddenly hire 200 new employees, you scramble to find adjacent space and rewire the whole floor. When a thief tries to break in, you hope the locks hold.

Now picture renting a floor in a premium managed tower. The building management handles fire suppression, backup power systems, and 24/7 security guards — so your business stays running no matter what happens in the wider building. If you need more space, the facilities team can expand your floor or move you to a larger one without shutting you down. The building is inspected and audited regularly to meet safety codes. The management company provides a dashboard so you can see exactly what's happening in your leased space at any time.

That's the cloud. The eight benefits covered in this section — high availability, scalability, elasticity, reliability, predictability, security, governance, and manageability — are the reasons why more organizations are moving their workloads into professionally managed cloud environments rather than running their own data centers.

## High Availability

<KeyTerm term="High availability">The ability of a system or service to remain operational and accessible for the maximum possible time, minimizing planned and unplanned downtime. In cloud computing, high availability is typically expressed as a percentage of uptime guaranteed over a given period, such as 99.9% or 99.99% per month.</KeyTerm>

High availability is about keeping your workloads running even when things go wrong. In a traditional on-premises environment, a single hardware failure — a failed network card, a crashed storage controller, a power surge — can take down your entire service. Cloud providers counter this by building redundancy into every layer of their infrastructure.

**How Azure delivers high availability:**

- **Multiple fault domains** — Azure distributes VMs and services across separate physical hardware within a data center (separate racks, power, network switches) so that a hardware failure doesn't take down all your instances simultaneously.
- **Availability Zones** — Physically separate data centers within the same Azure region, each with independent power, cooling, and networking. Services deployed across multiple Availability Zones can survive a complete data center failure.
- **Azure Service-Level Agreements (SLAs)** — Contractual uptime guarantees. Azure guarantees 99.9% uptime for a single VM with Premium SSD storage, 99.95% for VMs deployed in an Availability Set, and 99.99% for VMs deployed across two or more Availability Zones in the same region.

**What do those percentages mean in practice?**

| SLA | Maximum downtime per month |
|---|---|
| 99.9% ("three nines") | ~43 minutes |
| 99.95% | ~22 minutes |
| 99.99% ("four nines") | ~4.4 minutes |
| 99.999% ("five nines") | ~26 seconds |

<Callout type="tip">
The AZ-900 exam does not require you to memorize exact SLA percentages, but you should understand that higher availability SLAs require architectural choices — like deploying across Availability Zones — not just selecting a single resource. A single VM alone, even a large one, earns a lower SLA than the same VM deployed in a zone-redundant configuration.
</Callout>

## Scalability

<KeyTerm term="Scalability">The ability to increase or decrease computing resources to meet changing demand. Scalability is typically planned and deliberate — you add capacity when you expect growth, and you can scale down when growth slows. Unlike elasticity, scalability does not necessarily imply automatic, real-time adjustment.</KeyTerm>

Scalability means your infrastructure can grow (or shrink) to match your workload. It comes in two distinct forms — **vertical scaling** and **horizontal scaling** — and the AZ-900 exam frequently tests the difference.

### Vertical Scaling (Scaling Up / Scaling Down)

<KeyTerm term="Vertical scaling">Increasing or decreasing the capacity of an existing resource — for example, adding more CPUs, RAM, or storage to a single virtual machine. Also called "scaling up" (adding capacity) or "scaling down" (reducing capacity).</KeyTerm>

Vertical scaling means making an individual resource bigger (or smaller). If your application is running on a VM with 4 CPUs and 16 GB of RAM and it's struggling to keep up with demand, you scale up to a VM with 16 CPUs and 64 GB of RAM. You're not adding more VMs — you're making the one you have more powerful.

- **Scale up:** Increase CPU, RAM, or storage on an existing resource
- **Scale down:** Reduce CPU, RAM, or storage to save costs when demand drops

**Limitation:** Every resource has a maximum size. You can only scale a single VM up so far before you hit hardware limits. When you reach that ceiling, you need horizontal scaling.

### Horizontal Scaling (Scaling Out / Scaling In)

<KeyTerm term="Horizontal scaling">Adding more instances of a resource to distribute workload across multiple machines — for example, adding more virtual machines behind a load balancer. Also called "scaling out" (adding instances) or "scaling in" (removing instances).</KeyTerm>

Horizontal scaling means adding more instances — more VMs, more containers, more nodes — and distributing work across all of them. If your web application is overwhelmed, you add five more VM instances behind a load balancer so each instance handles a portion of the traffic.

- **Scale out:** Add more instances to handle increased demand
- **Scale in:** Remove instances when demand drops to reduce cost

**Advantage:** Theoretically unlimited — cloud providers have vast pools of resources. You're not constrained by the maximum size of any single machine.

<Callout type="tip">
A memory trick for the exam: **Up/Down = Vertical** (think of a single tower getting taller or shorter). **Out/In = Horizontal** (think of many buildings spreading across a city block). The exam may use any of these terms, so know all four.
</Callout>

**How Azure delivers scalability:**

- Azure Virtual Machine Scale Sets automatically increase or decrease the number of VM instances based on demand or a schedule
- Azure SQL Database can be scaled vertically by changing its service tier (DTU or vCore count)
- Azure App Service allows both vertical scaling (changing the App Service Plan size) and horizontal scaling (adding instances)

## Elasticity

<KeyTerm term="Elasticity">The ability of a system to automatically and rapidly scale resources up or down in real time in response to demand — and to release those resources just as quickly when they are no longer needed. Elasticity is automatic, dynamic, and immediate, in contrast to scalability, which can be planned and manual.</KeyTerm>

Elasticity is scalability on autopilot. The difference is subtle but important for the exam:

- **Scalability** — I decide to add more servers because next quarter looks busy. I can do this manually or automatically.
- **Elasticity** — My system detects that traffic just spiked 400% in the last five minutes and automatically provisions 20 more server instances right now, then removes them 45 minutes later when traffic returns to normal.

Think of elasticity as the managed office tower that can instantly grow your floor to three times its size the moment 500 extra guests arrive for your company conference — and then shrink it back down to normal size when they leave, charging you only for the expanded space while it was in use.

**How Azure delivers elasticity:**

- **Azure Virtual Machine Scale Sets** with auto-scale rules configured to add or remove VM instances based on CPU utilization, memory pressure, queue depth, or custom metrics
- **Azure App Service auto-scale** adds app instances when request queues grow and removes them when traffic subsides
- **Azure Kubernetes Service (AKS)** scales container pods (and even the underlying nodes) automatically based on CPU/memory demands
- **Azure Functions (serverless)** scales from zero to thousands of concurrent executions in seconds, with no configuration needed — the platform handles scaling entirely

<Callout type="info">
Elasticity is especially valuable for workloads with unpredictable or spiky traffic patterns — an e-commerce site during Black Friday, a ticket sales platform during a major concert announcement, or a tax preparation service in April. Without elasticity, you'd either over-provision capacity (waste money year-round) or under-provision it (crash during peak demand).
</Callout>

## Reliability

<KeyTerm term="Reliability">The ability of a system to recover from failures and continue to function correctly. A reliable system anticipates failures, minimizes their impact, and can restore itself to normal operation — often automatically — without requiring manual intervention.</KeyTerm>

Reliability is about what happens when things go wrong — and in large-scale computing, things always eventually go wrong. Hardware fails, software has bugs, networks drop packets, and natural disasters happen. A reliable cloud infrastructure is designed to survive all of these events without losing your data or taking your service offline.

Cloud providers achieve reliability through the **decentralized, distributed nature** of their infrastructure. Because Azure operates dozens of data centers distributed across the globe, a localized failure — a power outage in one building, a network issue in one region — does not bring down the entire cloud platform.

**How Azure delivers reliability:**

- **Globally distributed regions** — Azure has 60+ regions worldwide. You can deploy your application in multiple regions so that if one region goes offline, traffic automatically routes to another.
- **Availability Zones** — Three or more physically isolated data centers within each region ensure that a single data center failure doesn't affect zone-redundant services.
- **Azure Traffic Manager and Azure Front Door** — Route users to the nearest healthy endpoint automatically, failing over to backup regions in seconds if a primary region becomes unavailable.
- **Azure Backup and Azure Site Recovery** — Automated backup and disaster recovery services that protect your data and enable fast restoration after a failure.
- **Built-in redundancy** — Azure Storage replicates data at least three times within a data center by default, with options for cross-region replication.

<Callout type="info">
Reliability is closely related to high availability but they emphasize different things. **High availability** is about keeping the service running with minimal downtime (measured as uptime percentage). **Reliability** is about designing for failure — ensuring the system can detect failures, recover from them, and maintain data integrity throughout. A highly available system is also reliable, but reliability encompasses a broader set of concerns including data durability and disaster recovery.
</Callout>

## Predictability

<KeyTerm term="Predictability">The ability to forecast — with confidence — how a system will perform and what it will cost. Cloud predictability covers two distinct dimensions: performance predictability (the system will respond consistently under expected load) and cost predictability (you can anticipate your spending based on usage).</KeyTerm>

Predictability removes uncertainty from two areas that every business cares deeply about: performance and cost. The AZ-900 exam specifically calls out both dimensions.

### Performance Predictability

Performance predictability means you can rely on your cloud workload to respond consistently — the same response time, the same throughput — even as demand fluctuates or infrastructure events occur. Azure helps ensure performance predictability through:

- **Auto-scaling** — Resources automatically scale to match demand, so performance doesn't degrade as traffic grows
- **Load balancing** — Azure Load Balancer and Azure Application Gateway distribute traffic evenly across healthy instances, preventing any single instance from becoming a bottleneck
- **Azure CDN (Content Delivery Network)** — Caches content at edge locations worldwide, so users experience low latency regardless of geographic location
- **Consistent hardware and network** — Microsoft manages and continuously upgrades its global network and hardware, maintaining performance baselines that customers can rely on

### Cost Predictability

Cost predictability means you can accurately forecast your cloud spending — no surprise bills, no unexpected capital expenditures. Azure helps through:

- **Consumption-based pricing** — Pay for what you use. No upfront hardware purchases.
- **Azure Cost Management + Billing** — Real-time dashboards showing current spending, forecasted costs, and budget alerts before you exceed limits
- **Azure Pricing Calculator** — Estimate costs before deploying resources, so you can budget accurately
- **Reserved Instances and Savings Plans** — Commit to one or three years of usage at significantly reduced rates, making costs highly predictable and locked-in

<Callout type="tip">
On the exam, "predictability" questions often distinguish between **performance predictability** (will my app respond consistently?) and **cost predictability** (will my bill be what I expect?). Both are covered under the single "predictability" benefit. If you see a question about a company wanting to accurately forecast cloud spending next quarter, that is cost predictability. If a question is about ensuring a web application responds within a target response time under varying load, that is performance predictability.
</Callout>

<DeepDive title="Azure's Well-Architected Framework and Predictability">
Predictability is so central to cloud value that Microsoft built it into the Azure Well-Architected Framework — a set of guiding principles for building reliable, secure, efficient, and cost-effective workloads in Azure.

The framework's **Performance Efficiency** pillar directly addresses performance predictability: design your workload to scale horizontally, use auto-scaling, cache frequently accessed data, and choose the right service tier for your workload profile. When you follow these patterns, your application's behavior under load becomes predictable and testable.

The **Cost Optimization** pillar addresses cost predictability: right-size your resources, use reserved capacity for stable workloads, monitor spending with budget alerts, and eliminate waste (idle VMs, orphaned storage, over-provisioned databases). Organizations that implement the Well-Architected Framework typically report not just lower costs but more accurate cost forecasts — because the discipline of optimization also brings the discipline of measurement.

Azure Advisor, a built-in cloud recommendation tool, continuously analyzes your deployed resources and surfaces actionable recommendations across all five pillars. For cost predictability, it identifies underutilized VMs, recommends Reserved Instance purchases based on your usage patterns, and flags resources that could be downsized without impacting performance.
</DeepDive>

## Security

<KeyTerm term="Security (cloud benefit)">The comprehensive set of tools, controls, policies, and practices a cloud provider offers to protect workloads, data, and infrastructure from threats — while also enabling customers to implement their own security layers on top of the provider's baseline protections.</KeyTerm>

Cloud security is often misunderstood as a weakness of cloud computing. In reality, major cloud providers like Microsoft invest billions of dollars annually in security capabilities that the vast majority of organizations could never replicate on their own. The question is not "is the cloud secure?" but "who is responsible for which security controls?" — which takes you back to the shared responsibility model.

**What Azure provides as a security foundation:**

- **Physical security** — Biometric access controls, 24/7 security personnel, video surveillance, and strict visitor protocols at all data centers. You never need to worry about someone physically accessing your servers.
- **Network security** — Microsoft's global network uses DDoS protection, intrusion detection, and encrypted traffic between data centers by default.
- **Microsoft Defender for Cloud** — A unified security management and threat protection platform that continuously assesses your Azure workloads, surfaces vulnerabilities and threats, evaluates your security posture against industry benchmarks, and provides a security score with prioritized remediation steps.
- **Microsoft Entra ID** — Enterprise identity and access management with multi-factor authentication, conditional access policies, and privileged identity management.
- **Compliance certifications** — Azure holds 100+ compliance certifications (ISO 27001, SOC 2, PCI DSS, HIPAA, FedRAMP, and many others), which means workloads on Azure can inherit compliance posture rather than building it from scratch.

**What you still control:**

The cloud doesn't eliminate all security responsibility. Depending on your service type (IaaS, PaaS, or SaaS), you remain responsible for identity and access management, data classification and encryption, network configuration, and application-level security. The cloud gives you powerful tools — whether and how you use them is up to you.

<Callout type="warning">
Security in the cloud is a shared responsibility. The provider secures the underlying infrastructure, but you are responsible for configuring your resources securely. A misconfigured storage account that exposes private data publicly is not the cloud provider's fault — it is a customer configuration error. Azure provides tools to prevent and detect such misconfigurations, but you must use them.
</Callout>

## Governance

<KeyTerm term="Governance (cloud benefit)">The use of policies, standards, templates, and auditing tools to ensure that cloud resources are deployed, configured, and used in a consistent, compliant, and controlled manner — enforcing organizational rules across all teams and workloads.</KeyTerm>

Governance is about maintaining control and consistency across your cloud environment, especially as more teams and departments deploy their own resources. In a large organization, without governance, different teams may deploy resources in inconsistent ways — different naming conventions, different security configurations, in different regions, with different cost controls — creating a sprawling, unmanageable environment.

Azure provides powerful governance tools that let organizations define the rules once and enforce them everywhere, automatically:

- **Azure Policy** — Define rules that all Azure resources must comply with. Policies can audit existing resources, prevent non-compliant resources from being deployed, and automatically remediate drift. Example: "All storage accounts must use encrypted connections only."
- **Azure Deployment Environments** — Package policies, role assignments, and resource templates together into repeatable, auditable deployment configurations. Spin up an entire compliant environment from a single template definition.
- **Management Groups** — Organize subscriptions into a hierarchy and apply policies and access controls at the group level, cascading down to all subscriptions and resources beneath.
- **Azure Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)** — Grant users and services the minimum permissions they need to do their jobs, enforced consistently across all Azure resources.
- **Cloud Adoption Framework** — Microsoft's guidance for defining governance policies, security baselines, and cost management practices as you scale cloud adoption.
- **Tagging** — Attach metadata tags (Department, Project, Environment, CostCenter) to every resource, enabling granular reporting, cost allocation, and policy targeting.

<Callout type="info">
Governance is particularly important for organizations in regulated industries — finance, healthcare, government — where compliance with frameworks like PCI DSS, HIPAA, or ISO 27001 is mandatory. Azure Policy can automatically audit for compliance against these frameworks and generate audit reports, turning a manual, time-consuming compliance process into an automated, continuous one.
</Callout>

## Manageability

<KeyTerm term="Manageability (cloud benefit)">The ease with which cloud resources can be managed, monitored, and controlled. Manageability has two distinct dimensions: management OF the cloud (what capabilities you have to manage your resources) and management IN the cloud (the tools and interfaces through which you exercise those capabilities).</KeyTerm>

Manageability is one of the most nuanced AZ-900 benefits because it has two separate and distinct meanings that the exam tests explicitly. Always separate them in your mind.

### Management OF the Cloud

This refers to **what** you can do to manage your cloud resources — the capabilities and automation available to you:

- **Automatically scale** resources up or down based on demand
- **Deploy resources from templates** — provision identical, pre-configured environments repeatedly using ARM templates, Bicep, or Terraform, without manual configuration
- **Monitor resource health** and receive automatic alerts when metrics exceed defined thresholds (e.g., CPU above 90%)
- **Replace failing resources automatically** — if a VM becomes unhealthy, Azure can automatically terminate it and provision a replacement
- **Receive automatic alerts** and configure automated responses (e.g., scale out when CPU hits 80% for five minutes)

### Management IN the Cloud

This refers to **how** you interact with Azure to exercise those management capabilities — the tools and interfaces:

- **Azure Portal** — A graphical web interface (GUI) for managing all Azure resources visually, suitable for exploration, monitoring, and one-off tasks
- **Azure CLI** — A cross-platform command-line interface for scripting and automating Azure operations
- **Azure PowerShell** — A PowerShell module for managing Azure resources through PowerShell scripts, popular in Windows-centric environments
- **Azure REST APIs** — Programmatic access to all Azure management operations, allowing custom tools and third-party platforms to integrate with Azure
- **Azure Cloud Shell** — A browser-based shell environment (supports both Bash and PowerShell) that runs directly in the Azure Portal with no local installation required
- **Azure Mobile App** — Monitor and manage Azure resources from your smartphone

<Callout type="tip">
The exam distinction between "management OF the cloud" and "management IN the cloud" is specific and deliberate. **OF** = what you can do (scale, deploy, monitor, alert, replace). **IN** = how you do it (Portal, CLI, PowerShell, APIs, Cloud Shell, Mobile App). A question asking "what allows you to manage Azure resources from a smartphone?" is asking about management **in** the cloud (the Mobile App interface). A question about "deploying resources automatically from a template" is about management **of** the cloud (automated deployment capabilities).
</Callout>

<DeepDive title="Infrastructure as Code: Where Management OF and IN the Cloud Meet">
One of the most powerful combinations of cloud manageability is **infrastructure as code (IaC)** — the practice of defining your entire cloud environment in machine-readable configuration files rather than clicking through the portal.

With IaC, you write a template that describes every resource you need: virtual networks, VMs, databases, load balancers, firewall rules, monitoring alerts. You store that template in version control (Git). Then, any time you need to deploy an environment — for development, testing, staging, or production — you run the template and Azure creates everything automatically, identically, in minutes.

**Management OF the cloud** gives you the capability: you can deploy consistently from templates.

**Management IN the cloud** gives you the tools: ARM templates, Bicep, or Terraform (for cross-cloud) describe the resources; the Azure CLI or PowerShell initiates the deployment; the REST API drives the actual provisioning.

This combination means your infrastructure is reproducible (no "works on my machine" environment drift), auditable (changes go through code review), and recoverable (if an environment is accidentally deleted, you redeploy from the template in minutes rather than days).
</DeepDive>

## The Eight Benefits Together

No single benefit operates in isolation. A well-architected Azure solution combines all eight:

- A **highly available** and **reliable** architecture ensures the service stays up even when individual components fail
- **Scalability** and **elasticity** ensure the architecture can handle demand — planned growth and real-time spikes alike
- **Predictability** means the business can forecast performance and cost with confidence
- **Security** and **governance** ensure the environment is protected and compliant with organizational and regulatory standards
- **Manageability** brings it all together by providing the tools and automation to deploy, monitor, and control the entire environment efficiently

Azure's global distribution across 60+ regions, combined with Availability Zones, Azure Site Recovery, and Traffic Manager, ensures that regional failures do not cascade into total outages.
  </DiagramHotspot>

  <DiagramHotspot x="3%" y="35%" width="23%" height="12%" variant="layer" label="Elasticity — Automatic real-time scaling">
    Elasticity is automatic, immediate scaling in response to real-time demand changes. When traffic spikes, Azure automatically provisions additional instances. When demand drops, it removes them and stops charging for the capacity. Azure Virtual Machine Scale Sets, App Service auto-scale, AKS, and Azure Functions all deliver elasticity. It's the cloud equivalent of a managed building that can instantly expand or shrink your floor space to match your needs.
  </DiagramHotspot>

  <DiagramHotspot x="27%" y="35%" width="23%" height="12%" variant="layer" label="Scalability — Planned capacity growth">
    Scalability means you can grow (or shrink) your resources to meet changing demand. Vertical scaling adds capacity to an existing resource (scale up/down). Horizontal scaling adds more instances (scale out/in). Unlike elasticity, scalability can be planned and deliberate — you add capacity ahead of a known busy period. Azure supports both scaling strategies across most services.
  </DiagramHotspot>

  <DiagramHotspot x="52%" y="19%" width="23%" height="12%" variant="layer" label="Performance Predictability — Consistent response times">
    Performance predictability means your workload responds consistently under varying load conditions. Azure achieves this through auto-scaling (resources grow with demand), load balancing (traffic distributed evenly), Azure CDN (low latency for global users), and Microsoft's maintained, high-performance global network. Following the Azure Well-Architected Framework's Performance Efficiency pillar maximizes performance predictability.
  </DiagramHotspot>

  <DiagramHotspot x="76%" y="19%" width="23%" height="12%" variant="layer" label="Cost Predictability — Forecast your spending">
    Cost predictability means you can accurately forecast what you'll spend on cloud services. Azure enables this through consumption-based pricing (no surprise capital expenditures), Azure Cost Management + Billing (real-time spend tracking and budget alerts), the Azure Pricing Calculator (estimate costs before deployment), and Reserved Instances or Savings Plans (lock in rates for 1-3 years at significant discounts).
  </DiagramHotspot>

  <DiagramHotspot x="3%" y="62%" width="23%" height="12%" variant="layer" label="Security — Protection built into the platform">
    Security as a cloud benefit means Azure provides a comprehensive, enterprise-grade security foundation: physical data center security, network-level DDoS protection, Microsoft Defender for Cloud, Azure Active Directory (Entra ID), and hundreds of compliance certifications. Customers can add their own security controls on top. The shared responsibility model defines which layers are the provider's responsibility and which are yours.
  </DiagramHotspot>

  <DiagramHotspot x="27%" y="62%" width="23%" height="12%" variant="layer" label="Governance — Policy, compliance, and control">
    Governance means enforcing organizational rules consistently across all cloud resources. Azure Policy prevents non-compliant resources from being deployed and audits existing ones. Azure Deployment Environments package policies, roles, and templates into repeatable, auditable deployment configurations. Management Groups and RBAC enforce access controls hierarchically. Resource tagging enables cost allocation and reporting across teams, projects, and environments.
  </DiagramHotspot>

  <DiagramHotspot x="52%" y="62%" width="23%" height="12%" variant="layer" label="Manageability OF the Cloud — What you can do">
    Management OF the cloud refers to the capabilities available to manage your resources: auto-scale based on demand, deploy from templates (ARM, Bicep, Terraform), monitor health with metrics and alerts, automatically replace failing resources, and configure automated responses to events. These capabilities mean you can manage large, complex environments with minimal manual intervention.
  </DiagramHotspot>

  <DiagramHotspot x="76%" y="62%" width="23%" height="12%" variant="layer" label="Manageability IN the Cloud — How you do it">
    Management IN the cloud refers to the interfaces through which you interact with Azure: the Azure Portal (graphical web UI), Azure CLI (cross-platform command-line), Azure PowerShell (scripting for Windows environments), Azure REST APIs (programmatic access for custom tools), Azure Cloud Shell (browser-based Bash/PowerShell), and the Azure Mobile App (manage resources from your phone). All capabilities are accessible through all interfaces.
  </DiagramHotspot>

<KeyTakeaways>
- **High availability** keeps services running with minimal downtime, expressed as SLA percentages. Azure guarantees 99.9% for single VMs, 99.95% for Availability Sets, and 99.99% for zone-redundant deployments. Higher availability requires deliberate architecture choices.
- **Scalability** is the ability to increase or decrease resources to meet demand. **Vertical scaling** (up/down) resizes an existing resource. **Horizontal scaling** (out/in) adds or removes instances. Both can be manual or automated.
- **Elasticity** is automatic, real-time scaling — resources are provisioned the moment demand spikes and released the moment it subsides. Key distinction from scalability: elasticity is always automatic and immediate.
- **Reliability** means designing for failure — systems detect, recover from, and survive failures automatically. Azure's global distribution, Availability Zones, Site Recovery, and Traffic Manager enable geo-redundant, fault-tolerant architectures.
- **Predictability** has two dimensions: **performance predictability** (consistent response times under varying load, via auto-scaling, load balancing, and CDN) and **cost predictability** (accurate spending forecasts, via Pricing Calculator, Cost Management, and Reserved Instances).
- **Security** is a shared responsibility. Azure provides a comprehensive foundation — physical security, DDoS protection, Defender for Cloud, Entra ID, and 100+ compliance certifications — but customers remain responsible for data, identity, network configuration, and application security.
- **Governance** enforces organizational rules across all cloud resources. Key tools: Azure Policy (enforce and audit compliance rules), Deployment Environments (repeatable compliant environments), Management Groups (hierarchical policy application), and resource tagging (cost allocation and reporting).
- **Manageability** has two distinct exam-tested dimensions: **management OF the cloud** (capabilities — auto-scale, template deployment, health monitoring, automated alerts) and **management IN the cloud** (interfaces — Azure Portal, CLI, PowerShell, REST APIs, Cloud Shell, Mobile App).
- Scalability vs. elasticity is a common exam trap: scalability is the broad ability to scale (manually or automatically); elasticity specifically means automatic, real-time, demand-driven scaling.
- The manageability OF vs. IN distinction is specifically called out on AZ-900: OF = what you can do, IN = how you do it.
</KeyTakeaways>
