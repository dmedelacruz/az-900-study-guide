## What is Cloud Computing?

<KeyTerm term="Cloud computing">The delivery of computing services—including servers, storage, databases, networking, software, analytics, and intelligence—over the internet to offer faster innovation, flexible resources, and economies of scale.</KeyTerm> enables organizations to access technology resources on demand without owning physical infrastructure.

## Key Characteristics

- **On-demand self-service**: Provision resources without requiring human interaction with the service provider.
- **Broad network access**: Resources are available over the network and accessed through standard mechanisms.
- **Resource pooling**: The provider's resources are pooled to serve multiple consumers using a <KeyTerm term="multi-tenant model">An architecture where a single instance of software serves multiple customers (tenants), with each tenant's data isolated and invisible to others.</KeyTerm>.
- **Rapid elasticity**: Capabilities can be elastically provisioned and released to scale rapidly with demand.
- **Measured service**: Resource usage can be monitored, controlled, and reported transparently.

## The Shared Responsibility Model

In cloud computing, responsibilities are shared between the cloud provider and the customer. The division depends on the <KeyTerm term="service type">The category of cloud service being used — Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS), Platform as a Service (PaaS), or Software as a Service (SaaS) — each shifting a different amount of management responsibility to the provider.</KeyTerm> (IaaS, PaaS, or SaaS).

| Responsibility | On-premises | IaaS | PaaS | SaaS |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Data & access | Customer | Customer | Customer | Customer |
| Applications | Customer | Customer | Customer | Provider |
| OS | Customer | Customer | Provider | Provider |
| Infrastructure | Customer | Provider | Provider | Provider |

<DeepDive title="NIST Definition of Cloud Computing">
The National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) provides the most widely accepted definition of cloud computing in Special Publication 800-145. According to NIST, cloud computing is a model for enabling ubiquitous, convenient, on-demand network access to a shared pool of configurable computing resources that can be rapidly provisioned and released with minimal management effort or service provider interaction.

This definition underpins how Azure, AWS, and Google Cloud design their services and is frequently referenced in enterprise cloud adoption frameworks.
</DeepDive>

<Callout type="info">
Azure and other major cloud providers operate under the NIST definition of cloud computing, which identifies the five essential characteristics listed above. These characteristics distinguish true cloud services from simple hosted or managed services.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
Do not confuse the **shared responsibility model** with full provider responsibility. Regardless of service type (IaaS, PaaS, or SaaS), **you are always responsible for your own data and identity/access management**.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip">
For the AZ-900 exam, remember the mnemonic **"On Broad Resource Rapid Measured"** (On-demand, Broad network access, Resource pooling, Rapid elasticity, Measured service) to recall the five key cloud characteristics quickly.
</Callout>

<KeyTakeaways>
- Cloud computing delivers computing services over the internet, enabling on-demand access to servers, storage, databases, networking, and more.
- The five key characteristics are: on-demand self-service, broad network access, resource pooling, rapid elasticity, and measured service.
- The shared responsibility model divides security and management duties between the cloud provider and the customer — the split varies by service type (IaaS, PaaS, SaaS).
- Customers are always responsible for their data and access management, regardless of the service model.
- Moving to the cloud shifts infrastructure responsibilities to the provider, freeing teams to focus on applications and data.
</KeyTakeaways>
